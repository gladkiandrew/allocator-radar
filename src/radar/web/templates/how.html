How this works

Each allocator gets one readiness tier. A tier is not a score or an opinion. It comes from the rules below, applied to signals that a person has approved. Every signal cites a public document and a page.

Tiers are checked top to bottom, and the first match wins. The window is counted back from today, using the date printed on the source (the meeting, notice or filing date). The date we fetched the document is never used.

Which plans appear on the main table

A plan is listed only when our coverage of it is good enough to read: at least 3 documents fetched, at least one dated in the last 6 months, and its investment consultant identified from a cited document (config/main_table.yaml). Plans below that bar are on Coverage gaps, with the reason. Each plan's page also shows a coverage checklist covering 18 months of agendas and minutes, plus the latest IPS, quarterly report, actuarial valuation, audited financials and 112.664 disclosure.

Auto-approval

A pending signal is auto-approved only if all four hold. (a) It is not a termination, watch-list change or redemption; those always go to a person. (b) It was read from a parsed table, or its quote matches the cited page word for word. (c) Its date was printed in the source as a meeting, report or filing date (a quarter-end fallback does not count). (d) Its summary passes the language lint. Auto-approved signals set tiers and show as "auto-approved". They are never exported until a person approves them. Rules: config/auto_approval.yaml.

What counts

  • Only approved signals change a tier. New signals appear as pending review until a reviewer approves or rejects them.
  • A signal comes from one of two places. The extractor reads a fetched public document and quotes the page. Or a person enters a fact from a manual-only source, with its URL and date.
  • Nothing exports until it is approved. The CSV export contains approved signals only.

The Wall

  • No contact details anywhere. No model has an email, phone, address, or social-profile field. People appear only as name and title, as printed in public documents.
  • Scrubber first. Every piece of text is scrubbed before it reaches the language model, the search index or any table. Contact-shaped text becomes [REDACTED-CONTACT].
  • No outbound capability. The crawler sends GET and HEAD requests only, to an allowlist of hosts. It honors robots.txt and stops at any bot wall. The system cannot send email or messages, and it cannot submit forms.
  • Tested every night. Wall tests sweep every stored text column. They also run a canary packet full of fake contact details through the pipeline and require that none of it is stored.

Language

Summaries use factual verbs (reported, approved, voted, issued, awarded, terminated, placed on watch). A lint rejects evaluative words before anything is stored.
