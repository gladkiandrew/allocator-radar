How it works

From a board packet to a readiness tier

Each allocator gets one readiness tier. A tier is not a score or an opinion. It comes from the rules below, applied to approved signals. Every signal cites a public document and a page.

Phase C closes when all four are met. Criteria from ROADMAP.md.

01 · Discover

Find the plans

A registry of public plans and the pages where each one posts agendas, minutes and reports. A person approves it before any crawling.

02 · Fetch

Collect politely

GET-only, allowlisted hosts, robots.txt honored, 10 seconds between requests to a host, and a stop at any bot wall.

03 · Read

Extract signals

A language model reads each page and proposes signals, each with an exact quote. Contact details are scrubbed first.

04 · Verify

Check every claim

The quote has to appear on the cited page. Dates come from the document. A lint rejects evaluative language.

05 · Rank

Set the tier

Fixed rules turn approved signals into a tier. Only a person's approval lets a signal into an export.

Tiers are checked top to bottom, and the first match wins. The window is counted back from today, using the date printed on the source (the meeting, notice or filing date). The date we fetched the document is never used.

Which plans appear on the main table

A plan is listed only when our coverage of it is good enough to read: at least 3 documents fetched, at least one dated in the last 6 months, and its investment consultant identified from a cited document (config/main_table.yaml). Plans below that bar are on Coverage gaps, with the reason. Each plan's page also has a coverage checklist: 18 months of agendas and minutes, plus the latest IPS, quarterly report, actuarial valuation, audited financials and 112.664 disclosure.

Auto-approval

A pending signal is auto-approved only if all four checks pass. (a) It is not a termination, watch-list change or redemption; those always go to a person. (b) It was read from a parsed table, or its quote matches the cited page word for word. (c) Its date was printed in the source as a meeting, report or filing date. (d) Its summary passes the language lint. Auto-approved signals set tiers, but they are never exported until a person approves them (config/auto_approval.yaml).

The Wall

Contacts only behind the login

Business contact details that a public document prints are kept with their citation and shown only to signed-in members (D-B35). The public site shows none. Individual pay and identity numbers are never stored.

Scrubber first

All text is scrubbed before it reaches the language model, the search index or any table. Contact-shaped text becomes [REDACTED-CONTACT].

No outbound capability

The crawler sends GET and HEAD requests only, to an allowlist of hosts. The system cannot send email or messages, and it cannot submit forms.

Tested every night

Wall tests sweep every stored text column. A canary packet full of fake contact details goes through the pipeline, and none of it may be stored.

What counts

  • Only approved signals change a tier: approved by a person, or auto-approved under the four checks above. A new signal shows as pending review until it is approved or rejected.
  • Only verified signals are shown on this site, and only they set a tier here. verified against source: checked automatically, because its quote matches the cited page word for word or it was read from a table or a filing field. reviewed by a person: a person read the source page and approved it. Unverified, pending and rejected signals are never shown.
  • A signal comes from one of two places. Either the extractor reads a fetched public document and quotes the page, or a person enters a fact from a manual-only source, with its URL and date.
  • Summaries use factual verbs: reported, approved, voted, issued, awarded, terminated, placed on watch. A lint rejects evaluative words before anything is stored.
  • The CSV export contains only signals a person has approved.
